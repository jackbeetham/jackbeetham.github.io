About

I've always paid attention to the parts of a game most people never think about: the menus, the inventory screens, the prompts that ask "are you sure?" When they work, they disappear. When they don't, they're the first thing players complain about. That's what pulled me toward user experience.

I study computer science with a minor in human-computer interaction, which lets me work on both sides of an interface. I like starting with people, through interviews, surveys, and watching someone try a task for the first time, and then turning what I learn into designs I can build and test myself.

As a teaching assistant for Virginia Tech's HCI course, I lead design critiques and review students' usability studies every week. Giving that feedback has made me much sharper about spotting what's confusing in my own work.

Right now I'm focused on building new projects that bring design and code together, especially for games and VR.
